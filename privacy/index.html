Privacy Policy

Effective August 9, 2026

Service Break sends you notifications about live tennis matches. This policy explains what information the app handles and why. It's short because we collect very little.

This policy applies to everyone, everywhere. Service Break is available worldwide, and what follows describes how we handle your data wherever you are. Some countries give you additional rights on top of what is written here — where they do, those rights apply to you and we honour them.

Who is responsible for your data

SERVICE BREAK LLC is the controller of the information described here. You can reach us at contact@service-break.com. If your country requires us to name a person responsible for data protection, write to that address and it will reach them.

What we collect

No account, no name, no email. Service Break has no sign-up. The app creates an anonymous identity (a random ID from Google Firebase) so your settings can follow your device. We never know who you are.

Device and notification data. To deliver alerts we store: a random device identifier the app generates, your device's push-notification token (issued by Apple on iPhone and iPad, and by Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android), and your app settings — favorite matches, alert choices, quiet hours, snooze, and league filters — with timestamps.

The country you connect from. When the app contacts our servers, we store which country the connection came from — the country only, never a city, an area, or your IP address. We use it to know which privacy rules apply to you, because some countries require us to ask your permission before doing things that other countries do not. Our servers never see your IP address at all — Cloudflare works out the country at the edge of its network and passes us two letters.

Match data is public. Scores and match states shown in the app are public sports information; nothing about the matches comes from you.

Diagnostics. The app currently keeps a small technical log on your device only, to help us fix problems. It is not transmitted. If we ever enable in-app analytics, we will update this policy first.

How you use the app. When you arm a match, remove one, favourite a player, set a snooze or set quiet hours, we record that it happened — the action, the match it applied to, and the time. We do not record what you look at, how long you spend, or anything you type. This is kept for 180 days and it is how we decide which features are worth building.

In the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom we only do this if you have agreed to analytics. If you have not been asked yet, or you said no, we record none of it.

Separately, and wherever you are, we record the dates your device used the app. That is the same thing our server logs already show, and we keep it to understand whether people come back.

Website visitors

We count visits to service-break.com using Plausible, a privacy-focused analytics service. No cookies are set and nothing is stored on your device. Plausible records the page visited, the site that referred you, your screen size, and a country derived from your IP address — the IP address itself is not kept, and none of this identifies you. The data is processed in the EU, is used only to understand how the site is used so we can improve it, is never shared for anyone else's purposes, and feeds no advertising.

We do this because we have a legitimate interest in understanding how the site is used. You can object at any time — if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal we will not count your visit, and you can email us to object by any other means.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell your information. To anyone, ever.
  • We don't show ads or share data with advertisers.
  • We don't collect your name, email, contacts, location, or photos.
  • We don't track you across other apps or websites.

Who processes data for us

Seven service providers handle data solely to run Service Break. The app and this website are served by different companies, so it is worth being clear which does what.

For the app: Google Firebase (anonymous sign-in and push delivery), Apple (push delivery to iPhone and iPad), Expo (push delivery, and a check for app updates each time the app opens), Cloudflare (routes every request from the app to our servers, and works out which country it came from), and Render (our server and database hosting).

For this website: GitHub (hosts these pages, so it receives a request each time you open one) and Plausible (visit counts). Cloudflare is not involved in the website and Firebase, Apple, Expo and Render are not involved in it either.

Each receives only what its job requires.

Where your data goes

Wherever you are in the world, your data is transferred to and stored in the United States. We are a US company and our providers are US companies, except where noted below. If you are outside the United States, using Service Break means your data crosses a border.

Cloudflare runs a global network, so a request from the app reaches a Cloudflare server near you before being passed to ours. Your IP address is handled there and does not reach us.

Where the UK GDPR or the EU GDPR applies to you, those transfers are made under the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (and the UK Addendum) in our agreements with those providers. Website visit counts are the exception — Plausible is an EU company and processes that data on EU infrastructure.

Retention and deletion

Settings live as long as your device uses the app. Deleting the app abandons that installation's anonymous identity; the associated data becomes unreachable and is removed in routine cleanup. To request earlier deletion, email us with your request — since we can't identify you, we'll tell you what to send from the app so we can find the right record.

Your rights

Wherever you are, you can ask us to do these things and we will: give you a copy of the data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, stop using it, or send it to you in a portable form. You can also object to our using your data where we rely on our legitimate interest.

Depending on where you live, your local law may give you more than this — the EU and UK GDPR, US state privacy laws, Brazil's LGPD, Canada's PIPEDA, Australia's Privacy Act, India's DPDP Act and others each add their own. Where your law gives you a right we have not listed, you still have it.

One honest limit: we cannot identify you from your data. There is no account and no name attached to anything we hold. To act on a request we need the identifier shown in the app — and that identifier belongs to one installation on one device, so a request reaches the data for that installation only. If you use Service Break on more than one device, each has its own identifier. If you have reinstalled the app, the earlier installation's identifier is gone and we have no way to find its data — it is not linked to you, and it ages out on its own.

You can also object to our recording how you use the app. Email us with the identifier shown in the app and we will stop.

You can also complain to a regulator. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). In the EU it is the supervisory authority in the country where you live or work. Most countries have an equivalent authority and you are free to go to yours. You do not have to contact us first, though we would rather you did.

We aim to answer any request within 30 days, and will always answer within 90.

Your choices

Notifications can be turned off any time in the app or in your device's Settings. Everything the app stores about you exists only to deliver the alerts you asked for.

Age

Service Break is for adults. It is not intended for or directed at anyone under 18, or under whatever higher age counts as an adult where you live. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone below that age. If you believe a minor has used the app, email us and we will delete the associated record.
